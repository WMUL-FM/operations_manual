12. Technical Details

This part describes equipment in a detail that goes beyond what a board-operator needs to know to operate an air shift. The information is presented so that anyone interested in this equipment will be aware of its nature and capabilities.

You will be more confident as a broadcaster if you have an idea of what equipment is actually broadcasting your voice and the music out to the station’s listeners.

While long lists of specifications may be boring to non-engineers, the equipment behind those specifications is what enables you, the on-air announcer, to broadcast over WMUL‑FM. This means you have a personal stake in our transmitter and its peripherals. Whatever satisfaction and enjoyment you receive by working as a volunteer at WMUL‑FM depends directly on the equipment operating properly. If the antenna fell down or the transmitter blew up, you and the rest of us would be off the air.

To the listening audience, you are the heart and soul of WMUL‑FM. So please spend a few minutes with the brains and brawn of the station.

12.A. Transmitter Remote Control

The Burk ARC Plus Touch remote control system is a flexible broadcast transmitter control system. It allows for unattended and walk-away control for radio stations utilizing broadcast automation applications, as well as a full-time control system for stations which prefer live control.

Per FCC mandate (73.1350 and 73.1400), all broadcast stations must have complete control over their transmission equipment. In addition, each station must have accurate measurements demonstrating legal operation from its transmission network.

The Burk remote control gives WMUL‑FM control and metering of the transmitter’s operation. Control and metering are available at both the studio and transmitter room, via telephone, and via computer.

For the WMUL‑FM Operator, the Burk ARC Plus Touch is crucial in operating WMUL‑FM within FCC prescribed legal parameters.

The remote control features metering, status, and control channels. These grant the operator control over specific functions of the WMUL‑FM transmission network.

WMUL‑FM’s Burk ARC Plus Touch is configured as follows:

Metering Channels:

  1. WMUL‑FM transmitter room temperature (degrees Fahrenheit).

  2. The forward power of WMUL‑FM’s transmitter (watts).

  3. The calculated Effective Radiated Power (watts) from WMUL‑FM’s antenna. This channel also allows the operator to adjust the power level.

  4. The forward power of WMUL‑FM’s transmitter (watts), with the additional capability of turning the transmitter on and off.

  5. The Transmitter ambient temperature (degrees Fahrenheit), as measured by a digital thermometer in the transmitter itself.

  6. The input AC voltage of transmitter Power Supply Unit #1 (volts).

  7. The input AC voltage of transmitter Power Supply Unit #2 (volts).

  8. The input AC voltage of transmitter Power Supply Unit #3 (volts).

  9. The power reflected back into WMUL‑FM’s transmitter by imperfections in the transmission line and antenna (watts).

  10. The speed of transmitter Fan 1 (percent of maximum).

  11. The speed of transmitter Fan 2 (percent of maximum).

  12. The temperature of the Micro Controller Board inside the transmitter (degrees Celsius).

  13. The temperature of the Modulator inside the transmitter (degrees Celsius).

  14. The temperature of the Control Board inside the transmitter (degrees Celsius).

  15. The Transmitter ambient temperature (degrees Celsius), as measured by a digital thermometer in the transmitter itself. (This is the same reading as channel 5, just in Celsius.)

  16. The measured efficiency of the transmitter (percent).

Status Channels:

  1. Transmitter Room Door Open / Closed

  2. Transmitter General Fault / Clear

  3. Primary Audio Input / Backup Audio Input

  4. Transmitter On / Off

The Burk ARC Plus Touch uses a back-lit touch-screen LCD to display meter values and user interface. It displays a running five-second average for each channel. This running average keeps the display smooth and prevents frequent jumps in the channel reading.

All calibrations are the sole responsibility of the WMUL‑FM Engineering staff.

The touch-screen reduces the number of controls the operator needs to use. The average operator will only need to be concerned with the channel buttons (B), and the red and green "raise" and "lower" buttons (D and E).

The operator uses the up and down buttons (B) to scroll through the specific control channels. The "raise" (D) and "lower" (B) buttons are used with channel 4 to adjust the transmitter power. They are used with channel 6 to turn the transmitter on and off.

Remote Control Screen Layout

  1. Time (24 hr) [10:52:41]

  2. Channel up / down buttons with channel number in between. [4]

  3. Channel reading [ERP, 1350 Watt]

  4. Raise Button

  5. Lower Button

  6. Status Indicator [Exciter AFC OK]

Per WMUL‑FM’s operating guidelines, an operator must record a meter reading each hour, within a nine minute window at the end of each hour (:50-:59). 8.C.2. How to Take a Transmitter Meter Reading on Page 33 in Volume II

12.A.1. Interpolation Chart

The chart on the opposite page serves three purposes.

  1. It provides a written and posted record of the following data:

    1. The limits of WMUL‑FM’s authorized effective radiated power (1,260-1,470 watts or 90-105%), "ERP" for short

    2. The Transmission Line Efficiency (93.9% or 0.939), called "TLE"

    3. Antenna Power Gain (0.4611), called "APG"

    4. The limits of WMUL‑FM’s forward power out of its transmitter (2,910-3,395 watts), called "FP"

    With this data, one can use either the Forward Power (2) channel or Effective Radiated Power (3) channel to determine whether WMUL‑FM’s transmitter is operating within licensed parameters.

    ERP = FP * TLE * APG

    ERP = Forward Power * 0.939 * 0.4611

    If that value falls between 1260 and 1470, WMUL‑FM’s effective radiated power is within legal limits.
