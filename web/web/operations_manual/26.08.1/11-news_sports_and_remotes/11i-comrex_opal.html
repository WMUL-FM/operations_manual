11.I. Comrex Opal

The Comrex Opal is a web audio interface. A remote individual opens up an invite link in a web browser and it allows for a high quality audio connection between the station and the remote individual. It has been tested with Windows devices, Android phones, and the station’s iPads.

The unit has two channels, so two different callers can connect. Those are listed in the router as Opal 1 and Opal 2. It works via invite links, the same as one can invite someone to a Teams or Zoom meeting with a link.

The sports staff re-uses the same link to Opal 1 which is bookmarked on both laptops and both iPads. See 11.I.4. Managing Invites on Page 135 of Volume II for instructions on managing invites and see xref name="opal_single_caller" for how to use the Opal to bring in a single caller for an interview or other segment.

11.I.1. Compatible Hardware

PC: It can use an internal or USB webcam, an external USB Microphone such as the Rode NT USBs, or an external USB mixer such as the Tascam Mixcasts or the Yamaha MG12XU.

Phone: It can use the built-in microphone and speaker, or a bluetooth device such as the Tascam Mixcasts.

iPad: It can use either the build-in microphone and speaker, or connect to one of the Tascam Mixcasts over either bluetooth or a hands-free TRRS cable.

11.I.2. Using the Opal to Connect an Interview Guest

You can use the Opal to bring in an interview guest with a high quality audio. The guest will need access to a smart device or a computer with a webcam. If the guest does not have access to one of those things, you will have to conduct the interview over a telephone.

  1. Use the instructions in 11.I.3. Accessing the Opal WebUI on Page 134 of Volume II and 11.I.4. Managing Invites on Page 135 of Volume II to obtain an invite link for Opal 2. (Opal 1 is usually reserved for Sports.)

  2. Send the link to the interview subject.

  3. Route Opal 2 to the desired studio and phone connection.

  4. When the guest opens the link and clicks "Connect", they will automatically be connected through the Opal. You will be able to communicate with them through the studio.

11.I.3. Accessing the Opal WebUI

The WebUI permits the user to manage the invite links, check the status of each channel, and to drop a call and disable an invite.

  1. Double-click on the "Comrex Opal - Admin" icon on the desktop. The "Comrex Device Settings" page will load in Firefox.

  2. Login to the Opal. The credentials can be found in the group chat (Microsoft Teams). The "Invites" page will load.

  3. Below the "Control" menu item are indicators for each channel showing whether it is in use and, if so, which invite was used to connect to that channel.

11.I.4. Managing Invites

The Comrex Opal works via invite links. The invite link is generated in the WebUI and emailed to the guest. Each invite connects the guest to one of the two channels on the Opal. Channel 1 is usually dedicated to the Sports staff. One invite for that channel is reused for every broadcast. That invite is bookmarked on both laptops and both iPads.

Channel 2 remains open for interview guests, reporters, and any other individual connecting from outside the station.

Invites can be created, configured, and deleted from the Comrex Opal WebUI. It is possible to create mulitple invites for each channel.

  1. Access the Opal WebUI. 11.I.3. Accessing the Opal WebUI on Page 134 of Volume II

  2. Click the "Invites" menu item on the page. A second "Invites" page will load. This page lists all of the active invites. There are two in the screenshot: "Sports" and "Caller".

  3. Click one of the invites to open it for editing and to be able to copy the invite link.

  4. Click the "Add Invite" button to create a new invite and open it for editing.

    1. Link: Shows you the invite link. (It’s easier to use the "Copy Invite Link" button at the bottom.)

    2. Name: The name of the invite link, displayed on the previous page and at the top of this page. This is for internal use only. It will not be seen by the guest, but it will be seen by others at the station. Keep it professional.

    3. AGC: Whether Automatic Gain Control is enabled or disabled. This setting should be disabled for broadcast crews and enabled for guests.

    4. Channel: The Opal channel to which the invite will connect.

    5. Language: The language that the guest will see when they open the invite link.

    6. Enabled: Whether this invite is enabled or disabled.

    7. Copy Invite Link Click this button to copy the invite link to the clipboard. You can then paste it into an email or Teams message.

    8. Delete Click this button to delete the invite.

11.I.5. Dropping a Call and Disabling an Invite Link

If one of the invite links becomes publicly known, malicious actors may connect to the Opal in an attempt to disrupt our operations. In that case, it is neccessary to drop the call with the malicious actor and disable the invite, then delete the invite and create a new one.

  1. Access the Opal WebUI. 11.I.3. Accessing the Opal WebUI on Page 134 of Volume II

  2. Click the "Control" menu item on the page. The "Control" page will appear. This page shows which channels have a caller connected to them and permits the user to drop each call or drop the call and disable the invite.

  3. Click the "Drop Call and Disable Invite" button for the channel that has a malicious actor connected.

  4. Click "Back" and use the instructions in 11.I.4. Managing Invites on Page 135 of Volume II to delete the public invite and re-create it.

11.I.6. Troubleshooting the Comrex Opal (From the Studio End)

While the Comrex Opal is a wonderful device in many ways, it has a limited set of features for troubleshooting.

Remember that there can always be multiple problems and you need to tackle the problems one by one. Do not panic just because the problem was not fixed on the first attempt. Stay calm and work the problem.

11.I.6.a. They Can’t Connect! (Checking the Network at the Studio End)

If the remote crew is having trouble making a connection, the first thing to check is whether the Opal can talk to the internet.

The only network indicator on the Opal is the Ready light (2).

  • Dark: Network OK, no callers connected

  • Steady Green: Network OK, one or two callers connected.

  • Blinking Green: Device is booting

  • Red / Amber: Network Error

If the ready light is dark, then the problem is not in the studio. Continue with 11.I.7.a. We Can’t Connect! (Checking the Network at the Remote End) on Page 140 in Volume II

If the light is blinking green, give it another minute or two to finish booting.

If the light is steady green, check the WebUI to see if someone else (such as a different broadcast crew or a malicious actor) has connected to the call channel. See 11.I.3. Accessing the Opal WebUI on Page 134 of Volume II : item 3 discusses how to check whether someone is connected already. If a malicious actor is connector, see 11.I.5. Dropping a Call and Disabling an Invite Link on Page 137 in Volume II.

If the light is red or amber, the Opal is not going to work today. You will need to switch to using the Access, Linphone, or Gentner.

11.I.6.b. I can hear the remote crew, but they can’t hear me! (Audio Problems — Studio End)

The Opal does not provide any useable method of checking the audio levels going into it.

The only things to check are the routing and the phone module.

  1. Check that the source for "Opal 1d" (or Opal 2d) is set to "A Phn 1s" (or whichever studio and phone module you are using).

  2. Check the SuperPhone module in the audio console. Make certain that it is turned off and the appropriate caller module (Phone 1 or Phone 2) is in cue.

  3. Make certain that the "TB" knob is turned up. It should usually be at about "6", but try turning it up.

  4. Make certain that "MXM Source" is set to "AUD".

  5. Make certain that the Rivendell is in "PGM" and "AUD".

  6. Check the settings on the remote end. 11.I.7.c. The studio can hear me, but I can’t hear them! (Audio Problems — Remote End) on Page 144 in Volume II

11.I.6.c. The remote crew can hear me, but I can’t hear them! (Audio Problems — Studio End)

The Opal does provide a pair of indicator lights (1) to show the levels coming from the callers. Each light shows the level of one caller. It will blink green with good audio levels and turn red when clipping occurs. If the audio is clipping, tell the remote crew to turn down their audio.

If you have audio levels on the front panel, but you still cannot hear the remote crew, check the routing and the phone module.

  1. Check that the source for "A Phn 1d" (or whichever studio and phone module you are using) is set to "Opal 1s" (or "Opal 2s").

  2. Check the SuperPhone module in the audio console. Make certain that it is turned off and the appropriate caller module (Phone 1 or Phone 2) is in cue.

  3. Make certain that the "Cue" knob is turned up. It should usually be at about "5", but try turning it up.

  4. Make certain that the CR Microphones are all turned off.

If there are no levels on the front panel, the problem is on the remote end. Pick up at 11.I.7.b. I can hear the studio, but they can’t hear me! (Audio Problems — Remote End) on Page 142 in Volume II

If the remote crew has levels on their end, but you still do not have levels on the studio end, then have the remote crew try disconnecting and reconnecting, and then try rebooting their device.

11.I.7. Troubleshooting the Comrex Opal (From the Remote End)

While the Comrex Opal is a wonderful device in many ways, it has a limited set of features for troubleshooting.

Remember that there can always be multiple problems and you need to tackle the problems one by one. Do not panic just because the problem was not fixed on the first attempt. Stay calm and work the problem.

11.I.7.a. We Can’t Connect! (Checking the Network at the Remote End)

Checking the network connection will depend on the device type (Windows Laptop, iPad, or other) and the network connection type (wired or WiFi). This manual will only cover network troubleshooting for a Windows Laptop or an iPad, because those are the two types of devices owned by WMUL‑FM.

  1. Open a web browser and navigate to a major public website such as google.com, microsoft.com, or netflix.com . If these sites are accessible, but the Opal website is not, then the problem is probably on the studio end. If those sites are also inaccessible, then the problem is probably on this end.

Windows Laptop — WiFi

  1. In the lower right-hand corner, click on the WiFi icon.

  2. Look at the WiFi settings icon. If WiFi is connected, it should be blue and list the name of the WiFi network below it.

  3. Click the arrow to open the list of WiFi networks.

    1. Make certain that you are connected to the expected network.

    2. Make certain that the network that you are connected to says "Connected, secured" and does not say anything like "no internet".

    3. Try disconnecting and reconnecting. MU WiFi sometimes makes internal changes that require everyone to reconnect.

  4. Try rebooting.

  5. Find a network technician.

Windows Laptop — Wired Ethernet

  1. In the lower right-hand corner of the screen, check the network icon. If everything is connected, it should be an icon of a computer monitor with a cable to the right side.

    If there is no internet connection, it will be an icon of a globe with a slashed circle.

  2. Check the network cable at both the laptop end and the wall end.

  3. Try a different cable.

  4. Try a different network jack.

  5. Try rebooting.

  6. Find a network technician.

iPad

  1. Press the home button to return to the desktop.

  2. Scroll through the home screens until you find the "Settings" app and tap on it.

  3. Tap the "Wi-Fi" menu item on the left-hand side.

    1. Make certain that you are connected to the expected network. There will be a blue checkmark to the left of the name.

    2. Try disconnecting and reconnecting. MU WiFi sometimes makes internal changes that require everyone to reconnect.

11.I.7.b. I can hear the studio, but they can’t hear me! (Audio Problems — Remote End)

The exact steps to troubleshoot this problem will depend on which mixer you are using, which computer you are using, and which method you are using to connect the two.

Tascam Mixcast

  1. Check that the microphones are turned up and unmuted.

  2. Check that the master L and R channels on the right side of the Mixcast screen have levels from your microphones. Mute everything else and check the levels from the microphones.

  3. Check the microphone pre-amps.

    1. Tap the hamburger menu button to bring up the menu.

    2. Tap the first button that has a set of four icons: a microphone, a smartphone, a USB symbol, and a bluetooth symbol. The "Inputs" page will appear.

    3. Tap the button for the microphone. The "Level" page for that microphone will appear.

    4. Use the level control to adjust the pre-amp.

iPad and Tascam Mixcast — Handsfree TRRS Cable

  1. Tap the "Disconnect" button.

  2. Below the VU Meter on the Opal web page should be a "Microphone" control. Click the dropdown box next to it and select "Headset Microphone". If that is already selected, try selecting one of the other options and then switching back to "Headset Microphone".

  3. If either the "Microphone" control or the "Headset Microphone" option are missing, double-check that both ends of the handsfree TRRS cable are connected.

    1. One end should be plugged into the headphone jack on the end of iPad (bottom left-hand corner if the iPad is propped up in its stand).

    2. The other end should be connected to the "TRRS" jack on the back of the Mixcast. It has a smartphone icon above it and the label "(TRRS)" below it.

    3. The "Input Select" switch on the back of the Mixcast should be switched toward the Smartphone / TRRS jack.

  4. Try unplugging and replugging the handsfree TRRS cable from both ends.

  5. Try rebooting the mixer.

  6. Try rebooting the iPad.

  7. Reconnect when troubleshooting is complete.

iPad and Tascam Mixcast — Bluetooth

  1. Tap the "Disconnect" button.

  2. Below the VU Meter on the Opal web page should be a "Microphone" control. Click the dropdown box next to it and select "TASCAM Mixcast" (or Mixcast 2). If that is already selected, try selecting one of the other options and then switching back to "TASCAM Mixcast".

  3. If either the "Microphone" control or the "TASCAM Mixcast" option are missing, double-check that bluetooth is enabled and connected.

    1. Press the home button to return to the home screen.

    2. Scroll through the home screens until you find the "Settings" app and tap on it.

    3. Tap the "Bluetooth" item on the left-hand side.

    4. Make certain that bluetooth is on.

    5. Check the list of "My Devices" and make certain that "TASCAM Mixcast" (or "Mixcast 2") is "Connected".

  4. Try rebooting the mixer.

  5. Try rebooting the iPad.

  6. Reconnect when troubleshooting is complete.

Laptop and Tascam Mixcast — USB

  1. Below the VU Meter on the Opal web page should be a "Speaker" control and a "Microphone". control. Click the dropdown box for the "Microphone" control and select "Microphone (Mixcast 4)". If that is already selected, try selecting one of the other options and then switching back to "Microphone (Mixcast 4)".

  2. If either the "Microphone" control or the "Microphone (Mixcast 4)" option are missing, double-check the USB connection between the laptop and the Mixcast.

  3. Try unplugging and replugging the USB cable from both ends.

  4. Try rebooting the Mixcast.

  5. Try rebooting the laptop.

Laptop and Yamaha MG12XU USB Mixer

  1. Check that the microphones are turned up, unmuted, and in "ST" (Stereo).

  2. Check the microphone gain and compressor at the top of each channel.

  3. Check that the master "Stereo" channel is turned on and up.

  4. Below the VU Meter on the Opal web page should be a "Speaker" control and a "Microphone". control. Click the dropdown box for the "Microphone" control and select "Line (MG-XU)". If that is already selected, try selecting one of the other options and then switching back to "Line (MG-XU)".

  5. If either the "Microphone" control or the "Line (MG-XU)" option are missing, double-check the USB connection between the laptop and the Yamaha.

  6. Try unplugging and replugging the USB cable from both ends.

  7. Try turning the mixer off and back on again.

  8. Try rebooting the laptop.

11.I.7.c. The studio can hear me, but I can’t hear them! (Audio Problems — Remote End)

The exact steps to troubleshoot this problem will depend on which mixer you are using, which computer you are using, and which method you are using to connect the two.

iPad and Tascam Mixcast — Handsfree TRRS Cable

  1. Make certain that the volume on the iPad is turned up. The volume buttons on the iPad are on the top, toward the left-hand side if the iPad is propped up in its stand.

  2. Double-check that both ends of the handsfree TRRS cable are connected.

    1. One end should be plugged into the headphone jack on the end of iPad (bottom left-hand corner if the iPad is standing up in its case).

    2. The other end should be connected to the "(TRRS)" jack on the back of the Mixcast. It has a smartphone icon above it and the label "(TRRS)" below it.

    3. The "Input Select" switch on the back of the Mixcast should be switched toward the Smartphone / TRRS jack.

  3. Try unplugging and replugging the handsfree TRRS cable from both ends.

  4. Check the smartphone channel on the Mixcast. Make certain that it is turned up and umuted.

  5. Check the input level of the smartphone channel.

    1. Tap the hamburger menu button to bring up the menu.

    2. Tap the first button that has a set of four icons: a microphone, a smartphone, a USB symbol, and a bluetooth symbol. The "Inputs" page will appear.

    3. Tap the button for the smartphone. The "Level" page for the Smartphone input will appear.

    4. Use the level control to adjust the input level.

  6. Try rebooting the Mixcast.

  7. Try rebooting the iPad.

  8. Reconnect when troubleshooting is complete.

iPad and Tascam Mixcast — Bluetooth

  1. Make certain that the volume on the iPad is turned up. The volume buttons on the iPad are on the top, toward the left-hand side if the iPad is propped up in its stand.

  2. Double-check that bluetooth is enabled and connected.

    1. Press the home button to return to the home screen.

    2. Scroll through the home screens until you find the "Settings" app and tap on it.

    3. Tap the "Bluetooth" item on the left-hand side.

    4. Make certain that bluetooth is on.

    5. Check the list of "My Devices" and make certain that "TASCAM Mixcast" (or "Mixcast 2") is "Connected".

  3. Check the bluetooth channel on the Mixcast. Make certain that it is turned up and umuted.

  4. Check the input level of the bluetooth channel.

    1. Tap the hamburger menu button to bring up the menu.

    2. Tap the first button that has a set of four icons: a microphone, a smartphone, a USB symbol, and a bluetooth symbol. The "Inputs" page will appear.

    3. Tap the button for the bluetooth. The "Level" page for the Bluetooth input will appear.

    4. Use the level control to adjust the input level.

  5. Try rebooting the Mixcast.

  6. Try rebooting the iPad.

  7. Reconnect when troubleshooting is complete.

Laptop and Tascam Mixcast — USB

  1. Below the VU Meter on the Opal web page should be a "Speaker" control and a "Microphone". control. Click the dropdown box for the "Speaker" control and select "Speakers (Mixcast 4)". If that is already selected, try selecting one of the other options and then switching back to "Speakers (Mixcast 4)".

  2. If either the "Speaker" control or the "Speakers (Mixcast 4)" option are missing, double-check the USB connection between the laptop and the Mixcast.

  3. Try unplugging and replugging the USB cable from both ends.

  4. Try rebooting the Mixcast.

  5. Try rebooting the laptop.

Laptop and Yamaha MG12XU USB Mixer

  1. Check that the "USB In" channel on the mixer is on, up, and in "1-2" only (not in "ST").

  2. Check that the "Line / USB" button just below the RCA jacks is set to "USB".

  3. Below the VU Meter on the Opal web page should be a "Speaker" control and a "Microphone". control. Click the dropdown box for the "Speaker" control and select "Line (MG-XU)". If that is already selected, try selecting one of the other options and then switching back to "Line (MG-XU)".

  4. If either the "Speaker" control or the "Line (MG-XU)" option are missing, double-check the USB connection between the laptop and the Yamaha USB Mixer.

  5. Try unplugging and replugging the USB cable from both ends.

  6. Try turning the Yamaha USB Mixer off and back on again.

  7. Try rebooting the laptop.
